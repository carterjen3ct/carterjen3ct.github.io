Land Cover Change in Harrisonburg, Virginia, 1992–2019

This map tracks generalized land cover change in Harrisonburg, Virginia between 1992 and 2019 using data from the National Land Cover Database (NLCD). The goal was to make spatial patterns of growth and land conversion immediately readable at a glance, rather than requiring viewers to cross-reference two separate maps.

To achieve this, I created a custom classification scheme that assigns a unique color to each type of land cover transition while keeping land that stayed the same in 2019 the same color family: developed areas in grays and blacks, forest in greens, agriculture in yellows and oranges, bare land in browns, and water in blue. This approach lets you quickly see both what changed and what stayed stable across the city over nearly three decades. The map is projected in Virginia State Plane North. Created for GEOG 366 at James Madison University.

Methods: Land cover reclassification, transition matrix analysis, custom color symbology, change detection.
