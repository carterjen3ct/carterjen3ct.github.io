Sidewalk Suitability Analysis, Harrisonburg, Virginia

This suitability analysis identifies priority areas for new sidewalk infrastructure in Harrisonburg, Virginia. Using a 36x36-meter grid cell framework, I scored locations across the city based on a combination of spatial factors — proximity to key destinations, existing infrastructure gaps, land use patterns, and pedestrian demand — to produce a continuous surface ranging from least to most suitable for sidewalk investment.

The resulting map makes it easy to see at a glance where new sidewalks would have the greatest impact on pedestrian connectivity and safety. Areas around the JMU campus corridor show a concentration of high-suitability cells, reflecting the density of destinations and foot traffic in that part of the city. Created for GEOG 366 at James Madison University using City of Harrisonburg GIS data and real estate parcel data.

Methods: Raster-based suitability analysis, weighted overlay, grid cell scoring.
